Where the answers come from

Every figure comes from a table published by the Central Statistics Office (CSO), the State's official statistics body. This site holds a copy of about 13,000 of those tables, taken in October 2026.

When you ask a question, a computer program (a language model) looks for the tables that could answer it, reads what each one contains, and asks the database for the numbers. It then writes a short answer in plain words. The numbers in any table you see are exactly what the database returned. The program writes the sentences around them.

Under every answer you will find the CSO tables that were used, each with a link to the CSO's own website, and a section called “How this was worked out” that shows every step.

When it says it does not know

Much of what people want to know has never been counted, or was counted only in some years or for some places. When the tables do not hold what you asked, the answer should say so and offer the nearest thing they do hold. An honest “the data does not have this” is better than a guess.

How to check an answer

The program can make mistakes. It may pick a table that measures something slightly different from what you meant, or the wrong year. Before you rely on a number, open the source table on the CSO website and find the figure there. If an answer is wrong, please say so with the “No, something was wrong” button under it. That is how wrong answers get found.

What happens to your question

  • Your question is sent to {{ service }}, whose language model works out the answer. Do not put names or personal details in a question.
  • We keep the question and its answer, so that the answer has an address you can return to and so that we can find and fix mistakes.
  • We do not keep your internet address. To limit each person to {{ daily_limit }} free questions a day, we keep a code made from it that changes every day and cannot be turned back into the address.
  • This site sets no cookies and loads nothing from any other company. There is no tracking and no advertising.
  • To keep automated programs from asking thousands of questions, your browser does about a second of arithmetic on its own before a question is sent. Nothing about you is involved, and no other company is asked.
  • If you use your own API key, it is used for your question and then forgotten. It is never stored.

Who pays

Each answer costs the site a small amount of money. The site is free to use, within a monthly budget. When that budget is used up, free questions pause until the next month.
